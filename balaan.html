Balaan

Many narratives within Anitism are transmitted orally to subsequent generations, but many have also been documented in the form of scriptures. Oral literature consists of stories, that are passed down the generations by speech or song. All Philippine historical stories originated as oral literature. Stories naturally change and proliferate. Despite many recording projects, the majority have yet to be properly documented.

Scriptures

Scriptures are a collection of stories that explain the origins of the world, natural phenomena, and the actions of Diwata. It serves as a cultural narrative, often tied to the beliefs of a community. Anitism, on the other hand, encompasseses the spiritual practices, rituals, and theology rooted in those beliefs. Scripture is a component of religion, while religion is a broader system that includes worship and rituals. The stories are deeply tied to the Anitism religion, and its Diwata. While scripture focused on narratives about these beings, Anitism involves rituals to honor or appease them.


Creation

The world was without human beings except for a bird, Manaul, who was the pet of Laon. The bird was allowed to wander freely — scour the wilderness or dive to the bottom of the sea — on the one cruel condition that it would not soil its feathers.

As fate would have it, Manaul was caught in the thorny stem of the rattan tree. Try as it would, the bird could not get free and most of its colorful feathers were torn.

The Diwata Laon was furious. “Because of your misbehavior, I will make your life miserable,” Laon said. “Henceforth other creatures will disturb and frighten you. Out of your eggs will come living beings more powerful than you.”

And indeed the small fallen feathers of Manaul were transformed into flying birds and insects and from the large plumes came creeping animals. Mute with fright and despair, Manaul went to the hollow of the rattan tree and beheld the two strange forms of a man and a woman. Upon seeing them the bird died in an agony of despair. And man, woman and all other creatures took Manaul's place as the first inhabitants of Earth.


Laon of Mount Kanlaon

It is said that the divinity Laon dwells in a volcano (Mount Kanlaon) in Buglas island, the volcano hurls fire. They invoke Laon for their harvest; when she does not choose to grant them good harvests she sends the locusts to destroy and consume the crops. This Laon is a woman.


Makaptan of the highest sky

Makaptan dwells highest in the sky; they consider him a bad Diwata, because he sends disease and death among them, saying that because he has not eaten anything of this world, or drunk any earthen jars (rice wine), he does not love them, and so kills them.


Sidapa of Mount Madya-as

There is in the sky another Diwata, called Sidapa. This Diwata possesses a very tall tree on Mount Madya-as. There he measures the lives of all the new-born, and places a mark on the tree; when the person’s stature equals this mark, he dies immediately.


War and plundering

It is a historical fact that the worship of these Diwata was an important part of the formidable Visayan warrior culture. “When they go to war or on a plundering expedition, they offer prayers to Barangaw, who is the rainbow, and to their Diwata, Inaginid and Makanduk. For the redemption of souls detained in the inferno above mentioned, they invoke also their ancestors, and the dead, to see them and receive answers to their questions.”


The infernal regions

It is believed that at death all souls go directly to the infernal regions; but that, by means of the maganitos, which are the sacrifices and offerings made to the Diwata Pandaki in sight of the Mount of Madya-as, they are redeemed from Simuran and Siginarugan, Diwata of the lower regions.
