DIWATA

The following is a list of Diwata from the historical scriptures of Anitism.

DIVINITY

They perserve traditions in their customs; and in some songs that they retain in their memory and repeat when they go on the sea, sung to the time of their rowing, and in their merrymakings, feasts, and funerals, and even in their work, when many of them work together. In those songs are recounted the fabulous genealogies and vain deeds of their Diwata. Among their Diwata whom they designate one as supreme and superior to all others, Visayans call Laon, which denotes “antiquity.”

The definition of Diwata is Gods, ancestors, nature spirits and other entities. They also adored private idols, which each one inherited from his ancestors. The Visayans also called them Diwata. In memory of their ancestors Visayans kept small idols in their homes, which each one inherited from his ancestors. Idols were made of stone, wood, gold, or ivory. They adored animals and birds; and the sun and moon. Crocodiles were held in special veneration because of their obvious danger. They also attributed to the rainbow its kind of divinity. In the olden days, among their Diwata they reckoned also all those who perished by the sword, or who were devoured by crocodiles, or killed by arrows (which is considered a very honorable death), as well as those killed by lightning. They thought that the souls of such immediately ascended to the blest abode by means of the rainbow, called by them Balangaw. Generally, whoever could succeed in it attributed divinity to his aged father at his death.


DIVINE ENTITIES

Diwata of harvest:

  • Laon: Diwata who dwells in a volcano on Buglas island; controls the harvest.

Diwata of skies:

  • Makaptan: Diwata who dwells highest in the sky; sends disease and death among them.

Diwata of death:

  • Sidapa: Diwata who lives in the sacred Mount Madya-as; determines the day of a person's death by marking every newborn's lifespan on a very tall tree on Madya-as.

Diwata of redemption:

  • Pandaki: Diwata who is given ritual offerings so that a soul of the deceased will not be taken by the Gods responsible for torment in the afterlife.

Diwata of torment:

  • Simuran: tormentor Diwata.

  • Siginarugan: tormentor Diwata.

Diwata of war:

  • Balangaw: the rainbow.

  • Inaginid: war Diwata.

  • Makanduk: war Diwata.
