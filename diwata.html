DIVINITY

All that we have been able to learn has been handed down from family members from the older generation to the newer generation in tradition, and is preserved in their customs; and in some songs that they retain in their memory and repeat when they go on the sea, sung to the time of their rowing, and in their merrymakings, feasts, and funerals, and even in their work, when many of them work together. In those songs are recounted the fabulous genealogies and vain deeds of their Diwata. Among their Diwata is one who is the chief and superior to all the others, the Visayans call him Laon, which denotes “antiquity.”

They adored animals and birds; and the sun and moon. Crocodiles were held in special veneration because of their obvious danger. They also attributed to the rainbow its kind of divinity. In memory of their ancestors Visayans kept small taotao in their homes, representing the Diwata, namely: Gods, ancestor spirits, and possibly other spirits that represent or house them, which each one inherited from his ancestors. Taotao were made of stone, wood, gold, or ivory. Among their Diwata they reckoned also all those who perished by the sword, or who were devoured by crocodiles, or killed by arrows (which is considered a very honorable death), as well as those killed by lightning. They thought that the souls of such immediately ascended to the blest abode by means of the rainbow, called by them Balangaw. Generally, whoever could succeed in it attributed divinity to his aged father at his death.



DIVINE ENTITIES

Diwata of creation:

  • Laon: Diwata who is the chief; he is superior to all the others and also is the creator, residing in Mount Kanlaon.

  • Manaol: the first creature of Laon; a bird that is the pet of Laon.

Diwata of skies:

  • Makaptan: Diwata who dwells highest in the sky; they consider him a bad Diwata, because he sends disease and death among them. He does not love them, and so kills them.

Diwata of death:

  • Sidapa: Diwata who lives in the sacred Mount Madya-as; determines the day of a person's death by marking every newborn's lifespan on a very tall tree on Madya-as.

Diwata of redemption:

  • Pandaki: Diwata who is given ritual offerings so that a soul of the deceased will not be taken by the Gods responsible for torment in the afterlife.

Diwata of torment:

  • Simuran: tormentor Diwata.

  • Siginarugan: tormentor Diwata.

Diwata of afterlife:

  • Sumpoy: Diwata who leads them to Sisiburanen, before they go to inferno.

  • Sisiburanen: Diwata who keeps them all, when they go to inferno.

Diwata of souls:

  • Magwayen: Diwata that carries them to inferno.

Diwata of war:

  • Balangaw: the rainbow.

  • Inaginid: war Diwata.

  • Makanduk: war Diwata.
